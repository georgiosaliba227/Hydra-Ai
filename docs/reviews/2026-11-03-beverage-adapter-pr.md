Hydra now has a reproducible local Meta Muse Glimmer 30B image-adapter experiment for full beverage identity and printed container capacity. The selected real LoRA checkpoint scored **63/75 (84%) joint success** on the frozen held-out photo set, compared with 57/75 (76%) for the unadapted base. Exact printed capacity was correct on 73/75 (97.3%); joint success on natural backgrounds was 38/46 (82.6%).

The licensed cohort contains 250 distinct photos in 227 connected product groups, split 150 train / 25 validation / 75 test before inference. One epoch was selected using validation only; checkpoint, prompt, processor, runtime, policy and decoding configurations were frozen before test encoding. Both independent blinded AI identity reviewers must accept each full identity, exact capacity has no tolerance, and all 75 cases remain in the denominator. Nine paired cases improved and three regressed; these small-sample counts are not a population or causal improvement claim.

The implementation includes attributed collection/split tools, pinned Swift MLX vision and language workers, actual image-conditioned rank16 adapters on eight language layers, update/save/reload proof, strict cache/output/selection provenance and conservative identity/volume scoring. The selected checkpoint completed 150 real updates, trained 2,260,992 adapter parameters with a nonzero parameter delta and reloaded with zero tensor error. Raw photos, cached features and model weights remain outside Git; hashes and the artifact index identify them.

Validation: **1,284 pytest tests passed, 5 skipped, 58 subtests**; one existing FastAPI/httpx deprecation warning. The unchanged native binary passed 14 Swift tests and real-checkpoint preflights, followed by complete local baseline/adapter runs. A held-out review exposed a field-partition implementation bug; its generic regression fix implements the already-frozen policy, preserves all raw verdicts/references/volume rules, and leaves all development scores and checkpoint selection unchanged. Whole-source and focused independent code reviews found no remaining important defects.

Results and limitations: [experiment report](https://github.com/georgiosaliba227/Hydra-Ai/blob/codex/beverage-adapter-training/ai/beverage_training/reports/2026-11-02/README.md), [comparison](https://github.com/georgiosaliba227/Hydra-Ai/blob/codex/beverage-adapter-training/ai/beverage_training/reports/2026-11-02/final-comparison-v2-e1.json), and [artifact index](https://github.com/georgiosaliba227/Hydra-Ai/blob/codex/beverage-adapter-training/ai/beverage_training/reports/2026-11-02/artifact-index-v2-e1.json). Public images may have appeared in base pretraining; AI reviews are not a human population study, and nominal per-photo intervals do not adjust for product clustering or grader uncertainty. Median local language-worker latency was 14.19 seconds, excluding vision/model load.
